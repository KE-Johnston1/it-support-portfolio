# Case Study: Company-Wide Internet Outage

**Category:** Incident management and networking  
**Environment:** Simulated service desk  
**Skills illustrated:** Prioritisation, impact assessment, initial investigation, communication and documentation

> This is a learning exercise in a simulated environment. It is not a claim of handling a real business incident.

## 1. Scenario

The ticket queue contains several incidents. One reports that users at a site cannot access the external internet, while another affects a single user. A separate report says expected emails are missing.

## 2. Initial assessment

A site-wide internet outage should receive urgent attention because it may prevent many users from accessing external services and disrupt business operations. The initial priority should be based on impact, urgency and the organisation's incident process.

The missing-email report should not automatically be treated as low priority. It needs triage to establish the scope, timing and whether there are signs of a wider mail-flow or security issue.

## 3. Investigation in the simulation

1. Review the queue and identify the incident with the widest reported impact.
2. Open the simulated server-room view and inspect the ISP device.
3. Run the available ISP status check.
4. Record the reported outage status and estimated restoration time shown by the simulation.
5. Distinguish the reported external connectivity outage from an individual workstation fault.

## 4. Communication

Use an available internal communication channel to share a concise service update if the simulation indicates that internal communication remains available. Include the known impact, what has been checked and any estimated restoration time, clearly identifying it as an estimate.

Avoid promising a restoration time that has not been confirmed. Follow the relevant escalation or major-incident process if required.

## 5. Documentation

A useful incident note should record:

- Reported scope and business impact.
- Checks performed and the results shown.
- Whether an external provider outage is indicated.
- The restoration estimate, if provided.
- Who was updated and when.
- Any escalation, follow-up or service-restoration check required.

Do not close an incident merely because an external outage has been identified. Follow the process for monitoring, updates and closure, and record whether restoration was actually verified.

## 6. Verification and learning

**Evidence available:** The original exercise describes an ISP status check and an estimated restoration time. The actual service-restoration result is not established by this written scenario alone.

**What I learned:** Prioritisation should reflect business impact, not just the order in which tickets arrive. Clear communication and accurate notes help reduce duplicate reports and support continuity while the technical cause is being addressed.
